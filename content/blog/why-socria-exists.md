The first wave of AI products competed on the same axis: **how fast can we give you an answer?**

That race has a clear winner — the machines. They will always be faster than us at producing fluent text. What they cannot do, and should not pretend to do, is reason on our behalf.

## The cost of frictionless answers

When the cost of generating an essay drops to zero, people stop forming arguments. When the cost of generating an opinion drops to zero, people stop holding positions. When the cost of generating a decision drops to zero, people stop thinking through tradeoffs.

We don't lose intelligence overnight. We lose it gradually — by handing off the small reps that build it.

## A different bet

Socria is built on the opposite bet. The most valuable thing AI can do for you is **not** produce the answer. It is to ask the right question at the right moment so that you produce the answer yourself.

That's slower. It feels less productive in the first thirty seconds. But you walk away with the conclusion *and* the reasoning behind it — and the reasoning is what compounds.

## What we believe

- Human reasoning must remain central.
- Questions are more valuable than conclusions, especially under uncertainty.
- The best AI doesn't replace you. It helps you become more capable.
